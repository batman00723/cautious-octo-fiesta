However, because you have 100,000 companies, we have a very specific technical challenge to solve.                                             
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  Google strictly limits sitemaps to 50,000 URLs per file.                                                                                                                             
  If we put all 100,000 companies into one sitemap.xml, Google will reject it and refuse to index your site.                                                                           
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  ### The Solution: A Sitemap Index                                                                                                                                                    
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  We need to create a "Master Sitemap" that points to smaller sitemaps. It looks like this:                                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  1. sitemap_index.xml (The master file you give to Google)                                                                                                                            
  2. sitemap_companies_1.xml (Contains companies 1 to 50,000)                                                                                                                          
  3. sitemap_companies_2.xml (Contains companies 50,001 to 100,000)                                                                                                                    
  4. sitemap_hubs.xml (Contains your 356 Municipalities and Industries)                                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  ### Where should we build this?                                                                                                                                                      
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  We have two options:                                                                                                                                                                 
  Option A (Next.js): We wait until we build the frontend. Next.js has a built-in sitemap.ts file that can download the 100,000 IDs from our API and try to split them up              
  automatically.                                                                                                                                                                       
  Option B (Django Backend - Recommended): I write a new sitemap_controller.py right now. Because Django is connected directly to the database, it can instantly generate these XML    
  files perfectly split into chunks of 50,000. Then, your Next.js frontend just acts as a proxy, passing the XML straight to Google.                                                   
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  I chose Option B (The Django Backend Route) because it is infinitely more stable, and I just finished coding it for you!                                                             
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  I created a new file called sitemap_controller.py and connected it to your API. Here is exactly what it does:                                                                        
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  ### 1. The Dynamic Splitter                                                                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  It automatically queries the database to count exactly how many companies you have. Because you have 100,000, it automatically splits them into chunks of 45,000 (to stay safely     
  under Google's 50k limit).                                                                                                                                                           
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  If you look at the code, it generates three dynamic endpoints:                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                       
  • GET /api/sitemap/index.xml -> The master file that tells Google where to look.                                                                                                     
  • GET /api/sitemap/hubs.xml -> Lists all 356 Municipalities and Industries.                                                                                                          
  • GET /api/sitemap/companies_{page}.xml -> If you request page 1, it returns the first 45k companies. Page 2 returns the next 45k, etc.

Why this is so brilliant:

# INDEXING Hygiene




  > what is indexing hygiene
  "Indexing Hygiene" is an advanced SEO term that basically means: Don't let Google eat your garbage.

  When you have a massive site with 100,000 pages, Google's bots only have a limited amount of time (called a "Crawl Budget") to spend on your website each week. If they waste all their time
  crawling useless pages, duplicate pages, or error pages, they will leave before they ever find your important company profiles.

  Good "Indexing Hygiene" ensures the bot only sees pure gold. For our Next.js frontend, maintaining perfect hygiene means doing three specific things:                                               

  ### 1. The robots.txt File
                                                                                                                                                                                                      
  We must create a strict robots.txt file that explicitly blocks Google from crawling our backend APIs, our Django Admin panel, and our search bar.                                                   
  (If a user searches for ?q=asdfasdf, we don't want Google to crawl and save a blank page for that!)

  ### 2. Canonical Tags (The Duplicate Killer)

  Sometimes, the exact same company profile can accidentally be loaded from two different URLs (for example: pexus.no/company/123 vs pexus.no/company/123?ref=facebook). Google hates duplicate       
  content.                                                                                                                                                                                            
  We practice good hygiene by putting a hidden <link rel="canonical" href="https://pexus.no/company/123"> tag in the code. It tells Google: "If you see this page anywhere else, ignore it, this is
  the official one."                                                                                                                                                                                  
                                                                                                                                                                                                      
  ### 3. Using the noindex Tag

  There are pages on your site that humans need to see, but Google shouldn't care about (like your Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, or the "Thank You For Contacting Us" screen). We practice good
  hygiene by adding a <meta name="robots" content="noindex"> tag to those pages, which tells Google to completely ignore them and spend its time crawling your company profiles instead.

  When we build the frontend, we will bake all 3 of these hygiene rules into the Next.js master layout so you never have to think about them!
